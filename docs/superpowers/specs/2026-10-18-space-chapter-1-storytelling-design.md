# The space age, chapter 1: making it a story you can't stop reading

Follow-up to `2026-10-18-space-age-chapter-1-design.md`. Chapter 1 works and is
true, but every scroll looks the same: text on the left, the same globe on the
right, a number ticking. Freider: *"it is just the same visuals at every
scroll, which becomes boring fast"*, wants *images connected to each story*,
*the view of the Earth to change*, *as many journalistic tricks as possible*,
and *a better connection so the reader actually wants to continue*.

## The diagnosis

1. **No hook.** The page opens on a title and an explanation. Nothing makes
   you ask a question you need answered.
2. **No camera language.** One shot, held for 62 years. Film and good
   scrollytelling cut between wide, close, ground-level and detail shots.
3. **No faces, no things.** It's a story about people and machines with no
   picture of either.
4. **No threads.** Each beat ends and the next starts. Nothing planted early
   pays off later, and nothing tells you what's coming.
5. **No rhythm.** Every beat is the same length and shape, so nothing feels
   like a climax.

## The idea in one line

Treat chapter 1 as a documentary with five acts, a cold open, recurring
characters and a shot list, where the globe is the camera, not the backdrop.

## Structure

**Cold open (1995, Andøya).** The page opens on 25 January 1995, before any
title: a winter morning, a rocket leaves Andøya, a radar on the Kola Peninsula
picks it up, and the next day Boris Yeltsin says he opened his nuclear
briefcase for the first time. *Or so he said.* Then: *How did the sky get so
crowded that a science rocket could look like a missile? Start 38 years
earlier, with an empty sky.* The title appears only now. The open loop is
closed in act IV, when the story reaches 1995 again and the reader knows why.

**Five acts**, each opened by a full-screen title card with one number:

| Act | Years | Title | Card number |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 1957–1961 | The race | 0 → objects at end of 1961 |
| II | 1962–1969 | What it was for | objects at end of 1969 |
| III | 1970–1989 | Routine | objects at end of 1989 |
| IV | 1990–2006 | Business | objects at end of 2006 |
| V | 2007–2019 | Crowded | objects on 24 May 2019 |

**Threads that run through the acts (planted, then paid off):**

- **The witness.** Vanguard 1 is introduced in 1958 as the oldest object that
  will still be up at the end. Every act card checks in on it: *Vanguard 1 is
  still up there. It has gone round the Earth N times.* (N counted from its
  period). The globe briefly picks it out in each card. In the last beat it's
  still there, among 19,000 others.
- **The warning.** The 1961 explosion plants the idea of debris; Kessler names
  it in 1978; Fengyun and the 2009 collision cash it in. Each of those beats
  quotes or points back to the one before.
- **"Look up."** Echo's poem (*Thanks for making me look up*) is answered by
  the last beat: in 2019 people looked up in Leiden and saw a train of 60.
- **Andøya.** Ferdinand in 1962, then the cold open's rocket in 1995, then
  AISSat-1 listening over the Barents Sea. Norway's thread, never forced.

**Pull devices between beats:**

- **Last lines that lean forward.** Each beat ends on a fact that raises the
  next question, never a moral. (E.g. Telstar: *It had been launched into the
  belt Starfish had just filled.*)
- **A progress rail** on the right edge: the five acts as segments, the
  reader's position, and the next beat's title shown faintly (*Next: a bomb
  above the atmosphere*).
- **Variable rhythm.** Some beats become one sentence on a black screen (*The
  rocket rose about four feet.*). Long beats are allowed to be long. Act
  climaxes (1961 explosion, 2007 Fengyun) get extra screen time.

## The shot list: six ways to see

Each beat picks one main **shot**. The engine already has the data and the
globe; most shots are the same scene with the camera moved.

1. **Orbit** (what exists now, tightened): the globe from space, with camera
   moves: push in, pull back, look down on a pole, see the equator edge-on.
2. **Sky** (new): the camera stands on the ground at a real place and looks
   up, with twilight colours and the real objects crossing overhead, the same
   way the postcards show the stars over a ground station. Sputnik's rocket
   over Moscow; Echo over New Jersey; the geostationary ring low in the south
   from Andenes next to a Molniya high overhead; an Iridium flare; the
   Starlink train over Leiden.
3. **Archive** (new): a full-bleed photograph or document, slowly drifting,
   with a caption and credit. Faces: Korolev, Van Allen with Pickering and von
   Braun, Laika, Kessler, the Expedition 1 crew. Things: the Vanguard
   explosion, Starfish Prime's glow over Honolulu, Columbia's launch.
4. **Compare** (new): a before/after slider. TIROS's real first picture next
   to the famous mislabelled one; Hubble's blurred galaxy before and after
   1993; a Landsat place in 1972 and today; GPS error before and after 2 May
   2000.
5. **Number** (new): a full-screen figure that does the arithmetic for you.
   *51 → 365* the day the Ablestar stage exploded; *10,136 → 13,665* in one
   day in January 2007.
6. **Map** (new): a flat arc for things that never reached orbit or crossed
   a sea: Ferdinand going up from Andøya; the Black Brant's 24-minute arc
   towards Svalbard with a clock; Telstar's Andover–Pleumeur-Bodou link; ships
   lighting up in the Barents Sea when AISSat-1 starts listening.

Plus one **sound**: a play button for Sputnik's beep, if a free recording
exists.

## How it gets built (Claude orchestrates; Sonnet and Haiku build)

Claude decides what's built, writes each brief, and reviews the results.
Building is done by subagents:

- **Picture research** (Sonnet, running): free-licensed images per beat into
  `docs/space/images.md`.
- **Engine: shots** (Sonnet): a camera director that eases between shot
  specs per beat; the Sky shot (camera on the ground, wide field of view,
  twilight sky); the Orbit variants (edge-on, polar, push-in); the radiation
  belt torus; the debris-cloud spreading animation.
- **Story blocks** (Sonnet): act cards, Archive, Compare slider, Number, Map
  (simple SVG over the existing land data), progress rail, cold open, sound
  button. Server-rendered where it's text, so no-JS still reads it all.
- **Text** (Sonnet, then fact-check by a fresh Sonnet against the briefs):
  the cold open, act cards, rewritten beat endings that lean forward, the
  witness check-ins. Every new fact traced to a brief.
- **Assets** (Haiku): download the chosen images, make WebP sizes, record
  credits.
- **Final review** (the most capable model) across the whole branch.

The data, the counts, the honesty note and the no-JS reading all stay as
they are.

## Anti-goals

- Gimmicks that fight the reading: autoplaying sound, scroll-jacking that
  traps the reader, animations on every element.
- Invented drama: every cliffhanger is a true fact; the cold open says
  plainly that the briefcase story is disputed.
- Images without a free licence or a printed credit.
