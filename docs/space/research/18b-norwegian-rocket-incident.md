# 18b · The Norwegian rocket incident, 25 January 1995

## 1. What happened
- **21 December 1994:** the Norwegian Ministry of Foreign Affairs sent note ref. 21776/VII/94 to all foreign embassies in Oslo. It announced scientific launches from Andøya between 15 January and 10 February 1995, between 05:00 and 12:00 local time. The listed rockets were one **four-stage Black Brant XII** and two Viper 3A/Dart rockets, with impact zones out to the Svalbard area. Pavel Podvig published the text on russianforces.org in 2005.
- **25 January 1995, morning:** the Black Brant XII lifted off from Andøya Rocket Range (now Andøya Space). It carried NASA's **SCIFER** experiment ("Sounding of the Cleft Ion Fountain Energization Region"). SCIFER studied a fountain of ions flowing out of the dayside aurora (cusp) region. Partners included Cornell, NASA Marshall, the Universities of New Hampshire, Oslo and Tromsø, and the Norwegian Space Centre. Peak altitude was "nearly 1500 km" (NASA). The 1,453 km figure in Wikipedia comes from a now-dead EUCOM history page. The scientific paper reports crossing the ion fountain at about 1,400 km.
- **Launch time:** NRK (2012) gives 07:24, which is presumably Norwegian time (06:24 UTC). **I found no primary source for the UTC time.**
- The Olenegorsk early-warning radar on the Kola Peninsula tracked the rocket. According to Peter Pry's *War Scare* (1999), its profile looked like a US Trident missile, possibly an attack meant to blind Russian radar with a high-altitude nuclear burst. The alert went up the chain to President Boris Yeltsin, and the *Cheget* "nuclear briefcase" was activated. Secondary accounts say the decision process lasted about 8 of a nominal 10 minutes. The payload came down as planned near Svalbard, about 24 minutes after launch.
- **26 January 1995:** Yeltsin told reporters he had used his "black suitcase" for the first time the day before.

## 2. People
- **Boris Yeltsin:** Russian president; he publicly confirmed the briefcase use.
- **Mikhail Kolesnikov:** Chief of the General Staff, named by Pry.
- **Kolbjørn Adolfsen:** former director of the Andøya range, interviewed by NRK.
- **Peter Pry:** former CIA analyst, author of *War Scare*. He is the main source for the dramatic minute-by-minute version.
- **Geoffrey Forden:** MIT arms-control analyst. He argued Russia's early-warning satellites would have shown there was no mass attack.

## 3. Why it mattered on the ground
- It is the only publicly known case of a nuclear briefcase being activated. It happened four years after the Cold War ended, and was caused by an aurora-research rocket from Vesterålen.
- It showed how weak Russia's warning system was after 1991, and it changed how launches were notified. EUCOM history says notification procedures were redesigned afterwards.

## 4. Primary-source quotes
- "I used my black suitcase with the alarm button for the first time yesterday, which is carried with me wherever I go." This is Yeltsin, 26 Jan 1995, in an English translation quoted by the *Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists*, 13 April 2026. NRK's Norwegian version: "I går brukte jeg for første gang den svarte kofferten jeg alltid har med meg."
- "Alt var perfekt. Oppskytningen gikk som den skulle." (Kolbjørn Adolfsen, NRK, 26 Feb 2012)

## 5. Objects to highlight
- **None in the catalogue.** The Black Brant XII flew a suborbital arc, so it has no NORAD number and is not in satcat.csv. Show it as a single 24-minute arc from Andøya toward Svalbard, not as an orbit.

## 6. Commonly misstated
- **"Norway forgot to tell Russia."** False. The note went out on 21 December 1994. Why it didn't reach the radar crews is disputed. Pry's anonymous general blames the Foreign Ministry. Podvig thinks the note may have been registered, but nobody noticed this rocket was much bigger than earlier Andøya launches.
- **"The world was minutes from nuclear war."** This is contested. Forden argues Russia's satellites would have shown no attack. The *Bulletin* (2026) notes that Russian early-warning operators have said the *Cheget* was not activated. It also questions whether Yeltsin's statement was driven by the rocket or by politics.
- **"Three-stage rocket."** NRK says three stages; the ministry's note says four. Use four.
- **"A weather rocket."** Early wire reports called it meteorological; the Black Brant carried auroral and plasma physics instruments.
