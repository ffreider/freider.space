# 08 — Clarke's idea, Syncom 2 and Syncom 3 (1945–1964)

## 1. What happened
- **October 1945:** Arthur C. Clarke published "Extra-Terrestrial Relays: Can Rocket Stations Give World-wide Radio Coverage?" in *Wireless World*, pp. 305–308.
  - He showed that an orbit with a 42,000 km radius has a 24-hour period. A satellite there, over the equator, would stay above one spot.
  - His Fig. 3 shows that three stations could cover the globe.
  - A Clarke archive site says his submitted title was "The Future of World Communications".
- **Syncom 1**, 14 February 1963, went silent when its apogee motor fired.
- **Syncom 2**, launched 26 July 1963 on a Thor-Delta from Cape Canaveral, was the **first geosynchronous satellite**.
  - Its period was 24 hours, but the orbit was tilted 33°. So it traced a figure-eight, not a fixed point.
  - It was placed at 55° W over the Atlantic and Brazil, and began regular service on 16 August.
  - Capacity was one two-way telephone circuit or 16 teletype channels. Fully fuelled mass was 68 kg, and it was 71 cm wide.
- **23 August 1963:** Kennedy at the White House phoned Nigeria's Prime Minister Abubakar Tafawa Balewa through Syncom 2. Balewa was aboard USNS *Kingsport* in Lagos harbour.
- **Syncom 3**, launched 19 August 1964 from Cape Kennedy on a Delta D, was the **first geostationary satellite**.
  - It reached 0.1° inclination over 180° longitude (the Pacific) by 23 September.
  - It relayed live TV of the Tokyo Olympics (10–24 October 1964) to the United States. The Japanese end was the Kashima earth station, and the US receiving station was Point Mugu, California.
  - It was handed to the Department of Defense on 1 January 1965 and switched off in April 1969.

## 2. People
- **Arthur C. Clarke**, an RAF radar officer in 1945 and later a science-fiction author, set out the geostationary relay idea for engineers.
- **Harold Rosen** of Hughes Aircraft led the Syncom team with **Donald Williams** and **Thomas Hudspeth**. They designed a small spin-stabilised satellite that a Delta rocket could lift.
- **John F. Kennedy** and **Abubakar Tafawa Balewa** held the first two-way satellite call between heads of government.
- **Lyndon B. Johnson** recorded the message that opened the Syncom 3 Olympic TV link.

## 3. Why it mattered on the ground
- A geostationary satellite needs no tracking dish, since ground antennas can stay pointed at one spot. This geometry still carries most satellite TV.
- Americans watched the Tokyo Olympics live across the Pacific. NASA notes that Syncom 3 later carried US military traffic during the Vietnam War.

## 6. Commonly misstated
- **"Clarke invented the geostationary orbit."** Herman Potočnik ("Noordung") described a 24-hour station orbit in 1928. Clarke's contribution was the global relay network. He also imagined crewed "rocket stations", not small automatic satellites. Potočnik is from general knowledge here; cite a source for him if used.
- **"Syncom 2 was geostationary."** It was geosynchronous but inclined. Syncom 3 was the first geostationary satellite.
- **"Syncom 3 made the first transpacific TV."** Relay 1 relayed TV from the US to Japan on 22 November 1963. Syncom 3's Olympics coverage was the first live Olympic TV across an ocean.
- **"The Olympics went live to Europe via Syncom."** Johnson's White House release mentions plans to carry the coverage on to Europe. How it reached Europe (one secondary source says via Relay 1) is unverified.
