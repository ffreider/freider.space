# 19 · The International Space Station (1998–2000)

## 1. What happened
- **20 November 1998, 06:40 UTC:** a Proton rocket launched **Zarya**, the first module, from Baikonur in Kazakhstan. Khrunichev in Moscow built it as a temporary control module providing power, propulsion and communications. It was in orbit about nine minutes after liftoff (NASA, 2018).
- **4 December 1998:** Space Shuttle *Endeavour* (STS-88) launched from Kennedy's pad 39A carrying **Unity** (Node 1). Boeing built Unity at NASA Marshall. Two days later the crew captured Zarya with the robot arm and joined it to Unity; Wikipedia gives the docking as 7 December, 02:07 UTC. The two modules had never been fitted together on Earth.
- **31 October 2000:** the Expedition 1 crew launched on Soyuz TM-31 from Baikonur: William "Bill" Shepherd, Yuri Gidzenko and Sergei Krikalev. They docked on **2 November 2000**. People have lived on the station without a break since then.
- **Today:** the station weighs **419,725 kg**, measures **109 m** end to end, and has 1,005 m³ of pressurised volume (NASA facts page). It is the largest human-made object in orbit. Assembly continued for 13 years.

## 2. People
- **Bill Shepherd:** US Navy SEAL turned astronaut; commander of Expedition 1.
- **Yuri Gidzenko:** Russian Air Force pilot; commanded the Soyuz.
- **Sergei Krikalev:** flew on both STS-88 and Expedition 1. He was famous for being aboard Mir in 1991 when the Soviet Union dissolved.
- **Robert Cabana:** commanded STS-88. He and Krikalev were the first to enter the joined Zarya–Unity station.

## 3. Why it mattered on the ground
- Former Cold War rivals built and ran a station together. NASA's own words: "Former adversaries on Earth were now working together to build the largest laboratory in space."
- Five agencies from 15 countries run it. By 2018 it had hosted more than 2,500 experiments from more than 100 countries.
- It is the brightest artificial object in the night sky and can be seen from Norway with the naked eye. For many people it is the only satellite they can name.

## 6. Commonly misstated
- **"The first crew arrived in 1998."** The station was empty for almost two years because the Russian service module Zvezda was late. The first crew came in November 2000.
- **"Unity was the first module" or "the ISS started as an American station."** Zarya, built in Russia, went up first; Unity followed two weeks later. Zarya is often said to have been US-funded, but I did not verify that, so leave it out.
- **The commander's name** is often spelled "Shepard" (as in Alan Shepard). The Expedition 1 commander is **Shepherd**.
- **The size comparison:** the "football field" figure is the end-to-end span including the solar arrays, not the pressurised modules.
