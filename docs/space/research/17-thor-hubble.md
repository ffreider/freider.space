# 17 · Thor and Hubble (1990–1998)

## 1. What happened
- **24 April 1990, 12:33:51 UTC:** Space Shuttle *Discovery* (STS-31) launched from Kennedy Space Center with the Hubble Space Telescope. Hubble was released from the robotic arm on **25 April 1990** (NASA STS-31 page).
- **June 1990:** the images were blurred. The primary mirror's outer edge had been ground "2 microns too flat" (ESA/Hubble). The 1990 NASA Board of Investigation (the Allen report) traced the error to a test instrument, the reflective null corrector, that Perkin-Elmer used while grinding the mirror. A lens in it sat 1.3 mm out of position.
- **December 1993:** Servicing Mission 1 (STS-61, *Endeavour*) installed the COSTAR corrective optics and the WFPC2 camera.
- **18 August 1990:** BSB's broadcast satellite Marcopolo 2 launched from Cape Canaveral (it is in the catalogue under that date).
- **1992:** Televerket, the state telecom that later became Telenor, bought Marcopolo 2 in orbit and renamed it **Thor 1**. Romdirektoratet (the Norwegian Space Agency) and Store norske leksikon give only the year. One secondary source (Nordic Space, 2014) gives July 1992, and GlobalSecurity says "late 1992". Thor 1 was placed at about 1° West and broadcast TV and radio to Northern Europe "i 11 år" (Romdirektoratet).
- **Thor 2:** a Hughes HS-376HP satellite launched on a Delta II 7925 from Cape Canaveral LC-17A. **The date needs a decision:** the catalogue and most launch logs give 20 May 1997, 22:39 UTC. SNL gives 19 May, and Jonathan's Space Report no. 323 gives 21 May. Use "20 May 1997 (UTC)".
- **Thor 3:** launched 9 June 1998 at 20:35 US Eastern time, which was **10 June 1998 UTC** (Telenor press release, 10 June 1998). It added 14 transponders, cost Telenor about NOK 1 billion and was built in "knappe 12 måneder" (Romdirektoratet). It was the first satellite Telenor controlled itself; Telenor took over control from Hughes on 1 August 1998 (SNL).
- **Retirement:** Thor 3 was switched off and moved out of its working orbit in summer 2019 (Romdirektoratet). Gunter's Space Page says Thor 2 went to a graveyard orbit in January 2013. Thor 1, 2 and 3 are all still in orbit. The catalogue shows them all slightly above the geostationary belt.

## 2. People
- **Loren Shriver, Charles Bolden, Steven Hawley, Bruce McCandless II, Kathryn Sullivan:** the STS-31 crew. Hawley worked the robot arm that released Hubble.
- **Lew Allen:** chaired the board that found the cause of the mirror flaw.
- **Knut Reed:** CEO of Telenor Satellite Services at the Thor 3 launch.
- **Arve Johansen:** CEO of Telenor International in 1998.

## 3. Why it mattered on the ground
- Thor made Norway an owner of geostationary satellites. Canal Digital, the Telenor and Canal+ joint venture founded in 1997, sent its Nordic satellite TV from Thor 2 and 3 at 1° West. NRK channels are still broadcast from 0.8° West today.
- **Not verified:** I could not find a primary source saying Thor "brought Norwegian TV to the whole country", or naming which channels Thor 1 carried in 1992–97. Before that, Tele-X (launched 1989, a Norway–Sweden project) carried NRK to the North Sea and Svalbard (Romdirektoratet). Keep the "whole country" framing soft unless an NRK or Telenor source confirms it.
- Hubble's fix in 1993 turned an expensive embarrassment into the telescope that measured the expansion rate of the universe.

## 6. Commonly misstated
- "Hubble was ground wrong because of a math error." It was a misassembled test instrument (the null corrector), and warning signs during manufacture were not acted on.
- "Thor 1 was built for Norway." It was built for Britain's BSB and became surplus when BSB merged with Sky.
- "Thor 3 was deorbited in 2019." Geostationary satellites are moved up to a graveyard orbit; Thor 3 is still up there.
