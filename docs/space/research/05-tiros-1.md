# 05 · TIROS-1 and the first weather pictures, 1 April 1960

## 1. What happened
- **Launch:** 1 April 1960, 11:40:09 UTC (6:40 a.m. EST), from LC-17A at Cape Canaveral, on a Thor-Able (McDowell GCAT; NSSDCA).
- **Spacecraft:** an 18-sided drum 107 cm across and 56 cm high, mass 122.5 kg (about 270 lb). About 9,000 silicon solar cells and 21 nickel-cadmium batteries powered it. It spun at 8–12 rpm. It carried two 1.27 cm vidicon TV cameras, one wide-angle and one narrow-angle, and a tape recorder for pictures taken out of range of the ground stations (NSSDCA).
- **Ground stations:** Fort Monmouth, New Jersey, and Kaena Point, Hawaii (NASA Earth Observatory).
- **First picture:** taken at **13:31 UTC on 1 April 1960**, about two hours after launch. It showed a mostly featureless, cloud-covered disc (ECMWF 2010, citing NASA's original records via G. Anderson in *Weather* 65, 2010).
- **Working life:** 78 days. NSSDCA says it worked normally until **15 June 1960**, when a power failure ended useful TV transmissions. NASA Earth Observatory counts orbits from 1 April to 18 June.
- **Output:** "about 23,000" images (NASA Earth Observatory; Eisenhower, 17 August 1960). NOAA says **19,389 usable** (also given as "more than 19,000").

## 2. People

- **Morris Tepper**, NASA program scientist for meteorological satellites (NSSDCA).
- **H. I. Butler**, NASA Goddard, project scientist (NSSDCA).
- **RCA** built the spacecraft and cameras (several accounts; *I did not check this against a NASA primary source*).
- **T. Keith Glennan**, NASA Administrator, was congratulated by Eisenhower in his statement of 1 April 1960.

## 3. Why it mattered on the ground
For the first time, forecasters could see cloud systems spread across whole ocean basins, where few ships or weather stations reported. NASA notes that the early pictures showed spiral cloud bands around large storms, which proved their value at once. TIROS led directly to the operational weather satellites (ESSA, NOAA, GOES) that now give warning of hurricanes. NASA Earth Observatory writes that the TIROS programme "provided the first accurate weather forecasts based on data from space."

## 4. Primary-source quotes
- "Tiros I, the meteorological satellite which took 23,000 cloud cover pictures during its operating life of three months…" Eisenhower, Statement on U.S. Achievements in Space, 17 August 1960 (American Presidency Project, verified).
- The statement continues: "…provided increased assurance that revolutionary improvements in the science of weather forecasting will be achieved" (same source).

## 6. Commonly misstated
- **The famous "FIRST TELEVISION PICTURE FROM SPACE, TIROS I, APRIL 1, 1960" print is not the first picture.** NASA still publishes it under that caption ("TIROS' First Look"). ECMWF, reporting George Anderson's check of NASA's original records, shows it was taken on **2 April 1960 at 16:08 UTC**. The first frame (13:31 UTC, 1 April) was almost featureless. I compared NASA's file with ECMWF's illustration of the mislabelled image, and they are the same picture.
- **"First picture of Earth from space."** It is better described as the first from a weather satellite. Earlier pictures exist: from rockets from 1946, and Explorer 6 in August 1959 (*widely documented, but not re-verified this session*).
- **The image count of 22,952** circulates widely, but I found no source for it. Use "about 23,000" (NASA) or "19,389 usable" (NOAA), and say which one you mean.
- **End date:** both 15 June and 18 June appear. NSSDCA's 15 June is when useful TV transmissions ended.
