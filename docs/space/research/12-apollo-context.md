# 12 — Context: crewed flight, 1961–1969 (brief)

**Purpose:** a backdrop paragraph only. The story's subject is satellites; crewed flights were short visits.

## 1. What happened
- **Vostok 1, 12 April 1961:** Yuri Gagarin made one orbit in 108 minutes (NASA, ESA). He launched from Baikonur at 06:07 UTC; that time comes from Wikipedia only. He ejected and landed by parachute (NASA).
- **Through 1969:** US crewed flight ran through Mercury, Gemini and Apollo, and Soviet crewed flight through Vostok, Voskhod and Soyuz. None of those flights stayed in orbit long. Crewed craft left behind rocket stages and small debris; for example, NASA lists 34 fragments from the 1966 AS-203 S-IVB test.
- **Apollo 11:**
  - Launched 16 July 1969, 13:32:00 UTC, from Kennedy Space Center Pad 39A on a Saturn V.
  - Landed in the Sea of Tranquillity on 20 July 1969 at 20:17:40 UTC.
  - Returned 24 July 1969 at 16:50:35 UTC (all NSSDCA).
- **The Saturn V's spent stages:**
  - **First stage (S-IC) and second stage (S-II):** fell into the Atlantic. For Apollo 11, sources put the S-IC at about 560–660 km downrange and the S-II at about 4,200 km. The figures disagree, so check them in the AS-506 flight evaluation report.
  - **Third stage (S-IVB):** the Apollo 11 S-IVB "was injected into heliocentric orbit". The Apollo 12 S-IVB went "into Earth orbit", a loose, high orbit (NSSDCA). From Apollo 13 on, the S-IVBs were crashed into the Moon on purpose so the seismometers left there could probe the Moon's interior.
- **Eagle's ascent stage:** jettisoned 21 July 1969 at 23:41 UT, impact site unknown (NSSDCA). A 2021 paper by Meador argues it may still orbit the Moon. That claim is unconfirmed.

## 2. People
- **Yuri Gagarin**, first human in orbit, who never flew in space again.
- **Neil Armstrong, Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin, Michael Collins**, the Apollo 11 crew.
- **Wernher von Braun**'s team at NASA Marshall developed the Saturn V. This is general knowledge.

## 3. Why it mattered on the ground (for this story)
- Satellites carried the crewed era to the public. Intelsat relays sent Apollo 11 TV pictures abroad. Early Bird itself had been switched back on in mid-1969 to cover a failed Intelsat III (see brief 09).
- The stages that flew furthest left Earth's catalogue entirely. They are bookkeeping oddities rather than orbital debris.

## 4. Primary-source quotes
- "The Apollo 11 SIVB was injected into heliocentric orbit, the Apollo 12 SIVB into Earth orbit." NASA NSSDCA, "Impact Sites of Apollo LM Ascent and SIVB Stages".
- Armstrong's first words are well known but are not quoted here. The "a man" wording is disputed, so check the Apollo Lunar Surface Journal if the quote is used.

## 6. Commonly misstated
- **"Gagarin landed in his capsule."** No. He ejected and landed by parachute.
- **"All Saturn V third stages hit the Moon."** No. Apollo 8–12 stages went past the Moon, and only Apollo 13–17 stages were aimed at it.
- **"The Saturn V left big debris in Earth orbit."** Mostly not. The lower stages fell into the ocean. The 1968 Apollo 6 S-IVB did break up in low orbit, producing 16 fragments that all decayed within weeks (NASA ODPO).
- The object J002E3, found in 2002, is often said to be the Apollo 12 S-IVB. This is widely reported but was not verified for this brief.
