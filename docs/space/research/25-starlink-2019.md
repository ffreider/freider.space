# 25 · The first 60 Starlink satellites (24 May 2019 UTC)

## 1. What happened
- **Launch:** Falcon 9 lifted off from SLC-40, Cape Canaveral, at **02:30 UTC on 24 May 2019**, which was 22:30 EDT on 23 May. It carried **60 Starlink satellites** of about 227 kg each, roughly 13.6 t in total. That was the heaviest payload Falcon 9 had carried so far. The launch had slipped more than a week because of upper-level winds and a satellite software update.
- **Orbit:** The satellites were released at about 440 km and 53° inclination, then raised themselves toward about 550 km using krypton-fuelled Hall thrusters (Musk said this publicly on social media).
- **Design:** The batch is widely called "v0.9", meaning a pre-production design. I did not find a SpaceX primary page using that label.
- **The "train":** Around **22:55 UT on 24 May**, less than a day after launch, Dutch satellite tracker **Marco Langbroek** filmed the satellites from Leiden as a line of bright points crossing the sky. UFO reports followed across north-west Europe.
- **Astronomers:** On the night of **25 May**, **Victoria Girgis** at Lowell Observatory (Arizona) imaged the NGC 5353/4 galaxy group. Her frame was crossed by trails from more than 25 of the 60 satellites. On **3 June 2019** the **IAU** issued its "Statement on Satellite Constellations".
- **Scale planned at the time:** The FCC had approved **4,425** Ku/Ka-band satellites in March 2018 and **7,518** V-band VLEO satellites in November 2018, about **11,943** in total. In October 2019 the FCC submitted 20 ITU filings for SpaceX covering **30,000 more**.
- **What became of them (local SATCAT):** All 60 v0.9 satellites, plus 4 Falcon 9 debris objects, have re-entered. The first came down on 20 Feb 2020 and the last on 24 Oct 2022. By Oct 2026 the SATCAT lists 12,945 STARLINK objects, of which 11,153 are still in orbit.

## 2. People
- **Elon Musk** is SpaceX's founder and CEO. He announced the constellation and posted updates on the satellites' health after launch.
- **Marco Langbroek** is a Leiden-based archaeologist and satellite tracker. He predicted a search orbit before any official orbital elements existed and filmed the first widely shared "train".
- **Victoria Girgis** is a Lowell Observatory astronomer. Her streaked galaxy image became the IAU's illustration of the problem.
- The **International Astronomical Union** issued the statement. Its Commission B7 (protection of observatory sites) offered to work with operators.

## 3. Why it mattered on the ground
- For millions of people, the first sight of Starlink was a puzzling string of lights. It was the first time a satellite constellation became a public, visible event.
- For astronomy, it raised the prospect of streaks across wide-field survey images, such as those planned for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory, and of radio interference. That led to SpaceX's later darkening experiments and to the IAU's ongoing work on dark and quiet skies.
- It marked the start of the era when one company's satellites would outnumber everything else in orbit.

## 4. Primary-source quotes
- "Satellite constellations can pose a significant or debilitating threat to important existing and future astronomical infrastructures" *IAU, "IAU Statement on Satellite Constellations", 3 June 2019.*
- "this resulted in a spectacular view over NW Europe, when a "train" of bright satellites, all moving close together in a line, moved across the sky." *Marco Langbroek, SatTrackCam Leiden blog, May 2019.*

## 6. Commonly misstated
- **Date:** In the US it was 23 May (EDT). In UTC, and in the catalogue, it was **24 May 2019**. One outlet's "1430 GMT" is wrong. The correct time is 02:30 UTC.
- **"SpaceX filed for 12,000 satellites":** It got two FCC approvals totalling 11,943 (4,425 + 7,518). The 30,000 came later, as ITU filings in October 2019, not FCC approvals.
- **The Girgis image as typical:** The IAU/NOIRLab caption notes that satellite density is far higher just after launch and that the satellites dim as they reach their final orbits. The image shows a worst case.
- **"The IAU statement condemned Starlink":** The statement's text never names Starlink. Only its image caption ("Trails made by Starlink satellites") does.
- **"These satellites are still up there":** All 60 have re-entered.
