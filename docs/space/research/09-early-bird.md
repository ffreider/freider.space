# 09 — Early Bird / Intelsat I (1965)

## 1. What happened
- **Launch:** 6 April 1965 at 23:47:50 UTC (Wikipedia) on a Thrust-Augmented Delta (Delta D) from Cape Canaveral LC-17A. NASA ran the launch, and the Communications Satellite Corporation (COMSAT) paid for it.
- **Position:** geostationary over the Atlantic, at about 28° W (Wikipedia).
- **Capacity:** "240 voice circuits or one black and white TV channel" (ESA). It could not carry both at once.
- **Built by:** Hughes Aircraft, using the Syncom design. Mass figures conflict between sources: Wikipedia's text gives 34.5 kg and its infobox 149 kg. The smaller figure fits the in-orbit mass of a Syncom-class satellite, but I have not verified either.
- **First TV:** a multi-network special on Sunday 2 May 1965 carried live pictures between Europe and North America. The programme title "This Is Early Bird" comes from a TV Guide listing reported on a fan site, so it is secondary only. UN Photo has the Security Council sent "live" to Europe via Early Bird on 3 May 1965.
- **Commercial service:** began 28 June 1965. At 11:43 a.m. EDT President Johnson spoke via Early Bird to officials in six European countries, and heard replies from Wilson, Erhard and others.
- **Retirement:** retired January 1969. It was briefly reactivated in mid-1969 when an Atlantic Intelsat III failed, which coincided with Apollo 11. It was reactivated again in 1990 for its 25th anniversary. ESA says it outlived its planned 18-month life by two years.

## 2. People
- **Harold Rosen** and the Hughes Syncom team designed the spacecraft.
- **Joseph V. Charyk** was COMSAT's first president, and **Leo Welch** its chairman. COMSAT was the US company created by Congress in 1962 to run satellite communications commercially.
- **Lyndon B. Johnson** opened the commercial service.
- **Intelsat**, founded in August 1964, is the international consortium that owned the system. Johnson's 1965 speech called it "a joint venture of 44 countries". The founding date is from general knowledge; verify it.

## 3. Why it mattered on the ground
- Live TV and telephone calls across the North Atlantic could now be booked round the clock, because a geostationary satellite never sets. Telstar had only short passes.
- Early Bird carried part of the *Our World* global broadcast on 25 June 1967 (Wikipedia, not checked further).
- Intelsat grew into the global network that relayed the Apollo 11 TV pictures worldwide in 1969.

## 4. Primary-source quotes
- "For the first time a manmade satellite of earth is being put into commercial service as a means of communication between continents." Johnson, 28 June 1965. This is 22 words.
- "In these times the choice of mankind is a very clear choice between cooperation or catastrophe." Same speech.

## 6. Commonly misstated
- **"The first communications satellite."** No. It was the first *commercial* communications satellite in geosynchronous orbit. The NASA caption on the Commons photo calls it "the world's first communication satellite", which is wrong.
- **"It carried TV and phone calls together."** No, it carried either 240 voice circuits or one TV channel.
- **"Commercial service began on 6 April."** That was the launch date. Service began on 28 June 1965.
- **"Its 1990 reactivation marked its 40th anniversary."** It was the 25th.
- **Mass:** sources disagree (34.5 kg vs 149 kg), so don't print a mass until it is checked against Boeing or Intelsat records.
